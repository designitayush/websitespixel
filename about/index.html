01 Our story

WebsitesPixel started the way most studios do: one person, a laptop, and a client who needed a store that actually sold something. It has grown since — more than 150 Shopify stores, and a public rating of 5.0 across 52 verified reviews on our Shopify Partner profile — but the shape of the work has not changed much.

We build for founder-led brands. Hair and beauty, apparel, textiles, print, wellness. People who are running the business and the store at the same time, and who cannot afford a six-month engagement or an agency that disappears between invoices.

That is a specific customer, and it makes us a specific kind of studio. We are not trying to win awards for a brand with a nine-figure marketing budget. We are trying to build the store that takes a business from its first thousand orders to its first million in revenue — and then keep improving it.

The reason we exist is simpler than a mission statement. Most small ecommerce brands are sold either a cheap template that never converts, or an expensive rebuild they do not need yet. There is very little in between that is honest about which one you actually need. We wanted to be that.

Our mission

Beautiful is the minimum. We are hired to move a number — conversion, average order value, the time it takes you to publish a page — and we would rather be judged on that than on how the homepage looks in a portfolio.

How we work.

Seven stages. You know at every point what is happening, what you owe us, and when it goes live.

01

Discover — We start with your numbers, not your brief

Analytics, the funnel, the theme, and a conversation about what the business actually needs. Half the time the thing you came to us for is not the thing costing you the most.

02

Research — Your customer, your competitors, your category

What buyers in your category expect, where the competition is weak, and what your own visitors already do on the site you have.

03

Strategy — Scope, price and launch date, in writing

One document: what we are building, what it costs, when it goes live, and what is explicitly out of scope. Agreed before any design happens.

04

Design — The argument before the interface

We work out what the page has to persuade someone of, then design that. Two rounds of feedback, in a prototype you can click through.

05

Develop — Clean Liquid, no app sprawl

Built into the theme rather than bolted on with a $29-a-month app you will forget about. Staging store updated daily so nothing is a surprise.

06

Launch — Zero downtime, rollback ready

Cutover at your quietest hour, monitored for 48 hours, with a way back if anything looks wrong. You spend about thirty minutes on it.

07

Optimize — The store keeps earning after launch

Conversion experiments with written hypotheses, speed held to a budget month to month, and a report that says what we tried and what it did.

Before a line of Liquid

We argue about the page
before we build it.

Every section has to justify itself: what is it persuading someone of, and what happens to the sale if it is not there. Most of what gets proposed does not survive that question, which is the point — it is far cheaper to delete a section in review than in production.

The team.

Small on purpose. The people who scope your project are the people who build it, and you talk to them directly — there is no account manager relaying messages between you and whoever is actually writing the Liquid.

Design, front-end, Shopify development and conversion work sit in one room, so the argument about whether something is worth building happens before it gets built rather than after.

Two of our public reviews thank “Div” by name. That is the point — you get a person, not a ticket queue.

What we actually believe.

Six of these, and we will hold ourselves to them on your project.

Quality over shortcuts

There is always a faster way to make something look finished. We do not take it, because the store has to still be working in eighteen months when someone else is editing it.

Strategy first

We design the argument before the interface. If we cannot explain why a section exists, it does not get built.

Honest communication

If your idea will not work we say so on the call, not three weeks later in an invoice. If we are not the right fit we tell you that too.

Continuous improvement

A launch is the start of the work, not the end of it. The stores that grow are the ones that keep getting changed.

Long-term partnerships

Most of our work comes from clients who came back. That only happens if the first project was honest about what it would and would not do.

User-centred thinking

Your customer does not care about your brand guidelines. They came to buy something. Everything else serves that.
